# Accessibility standard and checklist

Target **WCAG 2.2 AA** and **EN 301 549** for participant-facing digital materials and delivery. These are delivery targets, not a claim that every third-party product is conformant. Ask privately what adjustment is useful; do not ask for a diagnosis.

## Before delivery

- [ ] Offer an adjustment request through a confidential channel (email, form, or named accessibility contact).
- [ ] Provide structured documents with real headings, meaningful link text, alt text, readable tables, sufficient spacing, and a plain-text alternative.
- [ ] Test slides, repository instructions, PDFs, forms, chat, captions, and the learning platform at 200% zoom and with keyboard-only navigation.
- [ ] Check contrast and do not use colour alone for status, severity, or correctness; pair colour with text, shape, icon, or pattern.
- [ ] Test screen sharing at readable size; avoid dense terminal text and flashing content.
- [ ] Provide captions and a microphone; repeat questions into the microphone; share a written transcript or key points.
- [ ] Keep focus order predictable; ensure all essential actions work without a mouse.
- [ ] Avoid auto-playing audio/video, rapid animation, and unnecessary motion; offer reduced-motion or static captures.
- [ ] Make a quiet seat/room and no-camera option available where feasible.
- [ ] Confirm accessible route, seating, power, restrooms, and remote breakout alternatives with the venue/platform.

## During delivery

- Announce agenda, breaks, objectives, and changes before they happen.
- Read important visual content aloud and describe the action on screen.
- Use plain language and one instruction at a time; put commands in copyable text.
- Pause after questions and allow written, spoken, or chat responses.
- Treat captions as support, not a reason to speak faster; correct important caption errors in chat.
- Offer a **solo alternative to pairing**: the same task, same evidence, helper check-in, and no penalty.
- Offer keyboard/menu alternatives for shortcuts and mouse gestures; do not take control without consent.
- Keep a predictable help protocol: raise hand/chat/private message; helpers respond without public diagnosis.
- Do not require eye contact, camera use, rapid turn-taking, or public self-disclosure.
- Use non-colour cues such as `Green / Amber / Red` labels and explicit text.
- Make sensory breaks normal: “You may step out and return at the next checkpoint.”

## Quiet and sensory considerations

- Keep notification sounds, background music, and room noise low.
- Avoid flashing, strobing, moving backgrounds, and unnecessary screen transitions.
- Provide a low-stimulation capture lane for participants who need fewer live changes.
- Let participants leave and rejoin without explanation; the run of show has resync moments.

## Confidential help template

> **Private support:** If you need a technical, language, accessibility, or safety adjustment, message `[named contact/channel]` privately. You do not need to share a diagnosis. We will agree the smallest useful adjustment and keep it confidential within the organizer’s approved support process.
